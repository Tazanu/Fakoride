Daylight The Fako Ride design system · v0.2 · rebuilt from research, 13 Sept 2026
Light ground, not dark

Near-white surfaces everywhere. A night variant exists for evening rides, and it is the exception.

This app is used standing at Checkpoint at one in the afternoon, two hundred kilometres from the equator. In bright light, readability tests light mode at 5.78 against dark mode's 4.29, and only 35% of people prefer dark outdoors. The first version got this backwards.

The interface ships in the phone's own font

Roboto on Android, San Francisco on iOS. No webfont for interface text.

Our riders are on Tecno, Infinix and itel handsets running Android Go with 2 GB of memory, paying by the megabyte. A bundled UI font costs APK weight and a flash of invisible text on exactly those devices. Roboto is already on the phone, already tuned for small sizes, and costs nothing.

One condensed face, for money and names only

Barlow Semi Condensed, two weights, subset. Fares, headings and the wordmark. Nothing else.

French runs 20–25% longer than English, and short button labels expand the most. A semi-condensed display face absorbs that, and a transit-signage grotesque suits a mobility product. Roughly 20 KB, spent deliberately.

Green is the action colour, and it had to be

Deep green for every primary action, verified badge and confirmed state.

MTN MoMo is yellow and Orange Money is orange, and both sit on the payment screen as brand chips. An amber or yellow call-to-action — which is what the first version used — competes with a payment brand the user is trying to choose between. Green is the only strong colour left that means nothing else here.

Thumbs, not cursors

Every target at least 48×48dp with 8dp between. Body text never below 14sp. Labels never below 12px.

Material's Android minimum, and the thing the first version broke worst: it had 9px navigation labels and 11.5px body copy, unreadable one-handed in sun on a 5-inch screen.

Dense with facts, single in action

Show prices, distances, driver history and demand up front. One primary button per screen.

Google's work on the next billion users found Western minimalism loses against the sensory density people here live in — sparse screens read as empty, not calm. But ride-hailing rewards the shortest possible booking flow. So: crowd the screen with information that removes a step, never with decoration.

Ratios are against white, computed rather than guessed. Everything clears the 4.5:1 WCAG floor for body text with room to spare, which is what daylight on a cheap LCD actually demands. The muted grey started at #6B7C73 and failed at 4.42:1, so it was darkened until it passed. Hill-rate amber (#8A5200, 6.4:1) appears as text only, never as a surface, so it can never be mistaken for an MTN chip.
